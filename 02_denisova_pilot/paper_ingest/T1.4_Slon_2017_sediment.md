Using targeted enrichment of mitochondrial DNA, we show that cave sediments represent a rich source of ancient mammalian DNA that often includes traces of hominin DNA, even at sites and in layers where no hominin remains have been discovered. By automation-assisted screening of numerous sediment samples, we detected Neandertal DNA in eight archaeological layers from four caves in Eurasia. In Denisova Cave, we retrieved Denisovan DNA in a Middle Pleistocene layer near the bottom of the stratigraphy. Our work opens the possibility of detecting the presence of hominin groups at sites and in areas where no skeletal remains are found." |

## Study scope (this paper introduces a class of samples, not a single specimen)

**Total samples collected:** 85 sediment samples from 7 archaeological sites in Eurasia.
**Age range:** ~14 ka to ≥550 ka.
**Sites with Neandertal mtDNA detected:** El Sidrón (Spain), Trou Al'Wesse (Belgium), Chagyrskaya Cave (Russia), Denisova Cave (Russia).
**Denisovan mtDNA detected:** one sample from layer 15, East Gallery, Denisova Cave (per source-locked quote below).

## v0 schema note (workflow learning)

This paper surfaces a schema question that the v0 SPEC doesn't yet answer: **do sediment samples get their own row in `specimens`, or a separate `sediment_samples` table?** Sediment samples share ~80% of the specimen schema (site, layer, chamber, chronology) but differ in `material_type` and `taxonomic_assignment` (a sediment sample can carry mtDNA from multiple hominin groups at once, unlike a bone).

**Proposed decision for v0:** Add `sample_class` enum to `specimens` (`bone` / `tooth` / `sediment` / `coprolite` / `other`) and allow `taxonomic_assignment` to be an array. Defer to schema-review checkpoint.
