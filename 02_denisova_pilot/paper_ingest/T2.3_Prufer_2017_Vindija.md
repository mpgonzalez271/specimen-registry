We sequenced the genome of a female Neandertal from ~50 thousand years ago from Vindija Cave, Croatia to ~30-fold genomic coverage. She carried 1.6 differences per ten thousand base pairs between the two copies of her genome, fewer than present-day humans, suggesting that Neandertal populations were of small size. Our analyses indicate that she was more closely related to the Neandertals that mixed with the ancestors of present-day humans living outside of sub-Saharan Africa than the previously sequenced Neandertal from Siberia, allowing 10-20% more Neandertal DNA to be identified in present-day humans, including variants involved in LDL cholesterol levels, schizophrenia and other diseases." |

## v0 schema note

Vindija 33.19 is the first **non-Altai** high-coverage Neanderthal in the corpus. Combined with Denisova 5 (Altai) and Chagyrskaya 8 (Altai) already in the corpus, the SAR now covers all three published high-coverage Neanderthal genomes.

The paper's finding that Vindija 33.19 is closer to the introgressing Neanderthal population than the older Altai specimens is important cross-specimen context that the current schema does NOT directly represent — it's a *relative-relatedness* claim between specimens.
