This individual derives from a population that lived before—or simultaneously with—the separation of the populations in western and eastern Eurasia and carries a similar amount of Neanderthal ancestry as present-day Eurasians. However, the genomic segments of Neanderthal ancestry are substantially longer than those observed in present-day individuals, indicating that Neanderthal gene flow into the ancestors of this individual occurred 7,000–13,000 years before he lived. We estimate an autosomal mutation rate of 0.4 × 10⁻⁹ to 0.6 × 10⁻⁹ per site per year, a Y chromosomal mutation rate of 0.7 × 10⁻⁹ to 0.9 × 10⁻⁹ per site per year based on the additional substitutions that have occurred in present-day non-Africans compared to this genome, and a mitochondrial mutation rate of 1.8 × 10⁻⁸ to 3.2 × 10⁻⁸ per site per year based on the age of the bone." |

**Verification note:** Byline (28-author) + abstract verified verbatim from PubMed. This paper is foundational for two orthogonal reasons: (1) it dates the Neanderthal-into-modern-human gene flow to ~50–60 ka using recombination decay on the long introgressed tracts in the 45 ka genome, and (2) it produces the field-standard modern-human mutation-rate calibration used in most downstream aDNA timing work. The Ust'-Ishim genome is the *oldest anatomically modern human genome* sequenced to whole-genome depth at ingest time.
