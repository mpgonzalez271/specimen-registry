By sequencing clones from short overlapping PCR products, a hitherto unknown mitochondrial (mt) DNA sequence was determined. Multiple controls indicate that this sequence is endogenous to the fossil. Sequence comparisons with human mtDNA sequences, as well as phylogenetic analyses, show that the Neandertal sequence falls outside the variation of modern humans. Furthermore, the age of the common ancestor of the Neandertal and modern human mtDNAs is estimated to be four times greater than that of the common ancestor of human mtDNAs. This suggests that Neandertals went extinct without contributing mtDNA to modern humans." |

**Verification note:** Byline + abstract verified verbatim from PubMed. This is the *foundational* paper of Neanderthal ancient DNA — the first ever Neanderthal DNA sequence, from the type specimen (Neander Valley, Feldhofer Cave). Note: the paper's "no mtDNA contribution to modern humans" claim was later shown to be correct for the mitochondrial line but the nuclear picture is more complex (Green 2010 showed 1–4% nuclear Neanderthal in non-African modern humans). This is a *reanalysis-affected* paper — the mtDNA claim held; the broader interpretive frame did not.
